# The next helpful reply

**HUDDLE sales and support desk · working SOP for review**

A person has asked for help. Our job is to understand the next useful step, take ownership and leave a clear record for whoever helps next. This guide covers questions received through the Enterprise and NFC enquiry forms. It does not introduce a support-ticket system or send messages automatically.

## Start your shift: what needs a reply?

1. Sign in at `https://www.huddleapp.in/sales/login` with your internal staff login. You do not need a customer organization. Change an initial password when required.
2. Review your assigned enquiries. Use Stage and Next follow-up filters to check New/Opened and overdue/upcoming work.
3. Open the enquiry and read its requirements, original message and timeline before replying. Opening records Opened; it does not mean a customer has been contacted.
4. If you cannot see a request, ask Super Admin to assign it to you. Staff see only assigned enquiries. Super Admin handles new, unassigned requests and team access.
5. Before leaving, record a next date for anything still waiting. Dates do not trigger automatic reminders; review your queue each shift.

Suggested working habit: acknowledge during your next staffed work period and agree a realistic next update. Do not advertise a response-time SLA unless management has approved it and the team can meet it.

## One inbox, three kinds of help

| What the person needs | Start here | Who owns the next step? |
|---|---|---|
| How to use HUDDLE / something is not working | Product help | Assigned colleague; escalate technical faults |
| Team rollout, package advice, demo or quote | Enterprise sales | Assigned salesperson; manager approves commercial commitments |
| Physical cards, artwork, compatibility, quantities or delivery | NFC enquiry | Assigned colleague; fulfilment/technical owner confirms specifics |
| Billing dispute, privacy request or possible account compromise | Sensitive escalation | Platform owner or designated business/privacy contact |

The form they used is a starting clue, not a verdict. A customer may use the Enterprise form simply to ask why a card will not open. Do not force them through a sales conversation to get help.

## The five moves

### 1 — Read what they already told us

Check the person's name, company, email, requested card-user count, NFC preference/quantity, timeline and original question. Use the reference to find their record. Avoid asking again for details already supplied.

For product help, request only what is missing: the affected public URL, what they expected, what happened and the approximate time/device. Ask for a redacted screenshot only when it helps. Never request a password, authenticator code or recovery code.

### 2 — Acknowledge the actual question

Adapt this; do not send an untouched template when the customer has explained something specific.

> Hi [name], thanks for explaining [specific issue or goal]. I’m [your name] from HUDDLE. I’ll help you with [next step]. To check this, could you share [one missing detail]? I’ll update you by [realistic time/date].

Send through the agreed channel. The sales workspace records activity; it does not send email or WhatsApp messages. Do not claim a confirmation or follow-up was sent unless you actually sent it.

### 3 — Help, qualify or escalate

**Product question:** give one useful action and the relevant How it works section. Ask them to try it, then check the outcome. Avoid a long list of unrelated instructions.

**Enterprise enquiry:** establish the main goal, actual card users, administrators, locations, rollout date and decision process. Separate digital subscriptions from physical hardware. Confirm the requested communication channel and the next agreed step.

**NFC enquiry:** confirm the quantity, new or existing cards, intended phones, branding/artwork and delivery location. Hardware, taxes, shipping, compatibility and delivery need confirmation before a quote/order promise. Never ask a customer to tap an unknown payment link.

**Escalation:** capture a concise reproduction and business impact. Ask the platform owner to involve the appropriate person through the team's agreed internal channel; add their name and the next update date to the timeline. Do not claim the app has reassigned a ticket to engineering—there is no ticketing workflow yet.

### 4 — Leave a useful breadcrumb

In **Log a conversation**, choose the real Communication channel, Outcome and Sales stage. Write the facts, the agreement and the next step. Follow-up and Demo requested require a next date. Check your local time before saving.

Use this note shape:

> [SUPPORT / SALES / NFC] · [what happened] · [what we did or agreed] · Next: [person + action + date]

Example notes:

- `[SUPPORT] Public card did not open on mobile. Exact URL received. Reproduced and escalated to platform owner. Next: Arun to update customer tomorrow at 11:00.`
- `[SALES] Called; customer did not pick up. No discussion took place. Next: retry tomorrow at the agreed office hours.`
- `[SALES] Customer requested a demo for 40 employees. Asked manager to arrange it. Next: confirm availability before promising a slot.`
- `[NFC] Customer needs 75 branded cards in Chennai. Artwork and delivery details pending. Next: obtain fulfilment confirmation, then send an approved quote.`

Keep notes factual. Do not add passwords, payment-card details, identity documents or unnecessary personal information. Use Internal note for work that involved no customer contact.

### 5 — Confirm the outcome

> Hi [name], [what changed / the answer]. Please try [one short check]. If that solves it, you’re ready to [their original goal]. If it still happens, reply with [one useful detail] and I’ll continue from here.

Record confirmation when received. If the issue is not resolved, retain ownership and agree the next update rather than silently closing the conversation.

## Which stage should I choose?

| Situation | Record it as |
|---|---|
| Received, unread | New (created by the system) |
| First opened | Opened (recorded when opened) |
| Actual conversation or reply | Contacted + the appropriate outcome/note |
| Customer asked for a later call / waiting for an update | Follow-up + agreed next date |
| Customer requested a demo | Demo requested + next date; use Demo arranged only once actually arranged |
| Confirmed sale and onboarding | Won / onboarded + factual note; no automatic account/subscription is created |
| Sales opportunity explicitly declined or closed | Closed / not interested + the actual reason |
| Product question resolved | Contacted + Internal note beginning `SUPPORT — resolved`, clear the outstanding follow-up, record customer confirmation |

**Support is not a deal.** Do not mark a helpful answer as Won, or a resolved bug as Not interested. The support-resolution note is an interim convention until a dedicated support workflow is built. Do not mark a person uninterested just because they missed one call.

## Small playbooks for familiar questions

### “My public card does not open.”

- Ask for the exact URL and reproduce it in a browser.
- Check for a mistyped organization/card name. Do not guess a replacement URL and claim it belongs to them.
- Ask their organization administrator to check publication and membership status. Staff cannot inspect other organizations' private settings through their sales login.
- Escalate reproducible failures with the exact URL, device/browser, time and visible message. Do not publish an inactive card or restore suspended access yourself.
- Record the fix and ask the customer to reopen the same link. Old reserved URLs should remain associated with the same card after a rename.

Reply starter:

> Hi [name], please share the exact card link and the message you see. I’ll check that address first so we can keep your existing QR and shared link working.

### “How do I change my public URL?”

Direct them to **My card → Set public URL**, or **Card personas → Public URL**. They can check a name and save it. Duplicate names inside one organization need a suffix; different organizations can use the same card name. Owners/Admins control the shared organization part. See `/how-it-works#your-link`.

### “I cannot edit this field / invite another person.”

Ask which organization is selected. Explain that company-controlled fields need their administrator and package limits apply to that organization. Ask them to review Upgrade & billing with the Owner. Do not change package allowances or promise an exception; escalate an approved commercial request to Super Admin.

### “I cannot sign in.”

First distinguish customer login (`/app/login`) from internal staff login (`/sales/login`). Check the address being used without asking for its password. Customer membership/invitation issues go to the organization Owner; unresolved account recovery goes to the platform owner through an approved identity-verification process. An internal colleague's password can be reset by Super Admin under Sales team. Never share an administrator login or bypass MFA.

### “Can I have a team quote or demo?”

> Hi [name], I understand you’re planning [goal] for [number] people around [timeline]. Are these the people who need digital cards, and do you also need physical NFC cards? Once we confirm that, I can arrange [approved next step].

Use current published package information. Record quantities, desired scope and the agreed next step. Only an approved quote establishes price, taxes, hardware, shipping and delivery. Do not promise discounts, payment activation, integrations, Wallet passes or implementation dates without confirmation. Mark demo requested before arranged.

### “We already have NFC cards. Will they work?”

Ask about the tags and intended devices. Compatibility needs confirmation; not every card/tag can be rewritten. Keep software access separate from hardware purchase. Collect the minimum artwork/delivery details through the approved business process when required, rather than putting sensitive documents in a general note.

### “I have a payment, cancellation or privacy concern.”

Acknowledge the specific request and send it to the platform owner/designated contact. Use the published policy pages and `info@touchmarkdes.com`. Do not promise refunds, erase records or change access yourself. Do not ask for full card numbers, banking passwords or OTPs. Record the issue category and escalation, excluding sensitive financial or identity data.

## Handover in four lines

> Customer/reference: [name + enquiry reference]  
> Need and impact: [one sentence]  
> Done so far: [checks, communication and promises]  
> Next owner/action/update: [person + task + time]

A timeline note does not notify a teammate. Make the handover through the agreed internal channel as well, and ask Super Admin to change the actual enquiry assignment if needed. Do not mark Won just to clear your queue.

## Before you finish

Can the next colleague see what the person needed, what actually happened, who owns the next action and when the customer expects an update? If yes, the record is useful. If not, add the missing sentence.

## Your team’s working agreements

Confirm staffed hours, the escalation owner and the approved business communication channel with your team lead. Use the current sales workspace to record activity and follow-ups; send replies through that agreed channel. If a request needs a separate support process, ask your lead how to hand it over.
